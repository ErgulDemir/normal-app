1 Overview

The Gaussian (normal) distribution is a continuous probability distribution in which values are spread symmetrically around the mean. Its bell-shaped curve shows that most values cluster near the mean and the probabilities taper off toward the tails.

Its probability density function is

\[ f(x;\mu,\sigma) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\, e^{-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}} \]

2 Key Characteristics

  • Symmetrical about the mean, so mean = median = mode.
  • Fully defined by two parameters, the mean (\(\mu\)) and the standard deviation (\(\sigma\)).
  • About 68% of observations lie within \(\pm1\sigma\), 95% within \(\pm2\sigma\) and 99.7% within \(\pm3\sigma\) (the 68–95–99.7 rule).
  • The total area under the curve equals 1.
  • The curve is asymptotic to the x-axis and extends from \(-\infty\) to \(+\infty\).

4 Applications in Psychological and Educational Measurement

  • Modeling test score distributions.
  • Standardizing scores (z, T, stanine, sten).
  • Defining confidence intervals and error bands.
  • Interpreting percentile ranks and probabilities.
